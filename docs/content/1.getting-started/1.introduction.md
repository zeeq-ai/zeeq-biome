## What it is

A **biome** is your app's entity graph in miniature: just the rows one test needs, wired together correctly. Zeeq Biome is a `.NET 10` / `C# 14` library that builds that slice for you.

- You declare one ***blueprint*** per entity type. It produces a **valid, uninteresting** default.
- A test builds its scenario as a short fluent chain that **mentions only the properties under test**.
- Parents and children are typed, numbers and ordering come from sequences, the clock is controlled, and persistence is pluggable (EF Core or none).

::tip
***Customize only the property under test.*** Everything else stays uninteresting, so a reader sees the point of the test at a glance.
::

## The payoff

A test needs an active code-review lock. Here is the setup against zeeq-app's real model shapes, written with and without Biome:

::code-group


## Why "biome"

An ecosystem is small, self-consistent, and has just the parts that interact. That is what a good test fixture is: not the whole database, only the entities that matter, in a valid relationship. The metaphor shows up in the vocabulary:

|Term|Meaning|
|---|---|
|**Seed**|The baseline every test starts from: an owner, an organization, whatever your app always needs.|
|**Blueprint**|The recipe for one entity type: a factory plus optional traits and derived defaults.|
|**Sequence**|A declared-once counter or rank, scoped per session, parent or ancestor.|
|**Anchor**|The typed parent the next Add attaches to. Every Add re-anchors to its own output.|
|**Store**|Where the finished biome is saved: EF Core, none, or your own `IBiomeStore`.|

## The one rule

***The call site wins, then the more specific default. Nothing overwrites.*** See [Customizing](/guides/customizing) for the full order.
